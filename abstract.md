Does mandated climate adaptation pay for itself? I study a 1994 wind standard reform that required structural upgrades to manufactured homes in hurricane-prone areas. Homes built under the reform command a price premium of roughly 4.0% in the most exposed counties, reflecting the capitalized value of resilience. However, surviving quantities in those counties fall by up to 53.2%, implying that the cost of the wind standard exceeded the private value buyers placed on it. I support this conclusion using claim and policy data from the National Flood Insurance Program, where I compare flood damage to manufactured and site-built homes of different construction vintages exposed to floods in the same county and over the same period. Manufactured homes built after 1994 experience a 15.4% reduction in building damage per claim relative to the change for site-built homes of the same vintage. The present value of these benefits is small -- only \$885 per insured home -- reflecting the low baseline values of insured manufactured homes. For manufactured-home owners, mandatory climate adaptation does not generally pencil out.
